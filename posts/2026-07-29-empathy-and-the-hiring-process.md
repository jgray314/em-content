# Empathy and the Hiring Process

Over the last two years, I've been a hiring manager, a candidate, and worked closely with recruiters. One observation keeps coming back to me: every participant in hiring believes the system is failing them—and they're all right.

What has surprised me isn't the frustration. It's how often that frustration gets mistaken for a lack of caring.

I don't think that's true.

Candidates submit hundreds of applications, often investing significant time tailoring resumes and researching companies, yet conversion to interviews is rarer than they’ve ever seen. And then when they finally do get interviews, it often feels like roles disappear silently and it isn’t clear whether the rejection is about anything they could control. Candidates increasingly feel like they're shouting into the void. 

Recruiters often take the blame, but consider how their job has changed over the last few years. Even ruling out completely fraudulent identities and misrepresented credentials, they are facing an unprecedented tsunami of inbound profiles of wildly varying quality. With the rise of Easy Apply and trivial AI customization they are triaging hundreds of applications. Instead of finding talent, they are trying to find the signal in the overwhelming noise. Recruiters are rewarded for filling positions, not maintaining relationships with thousands of candidates.

Hiring managers also play a key role in the dynamic. Each hire faces a higher degree of scrutiny, especially as companies examine how AI tooling should evolve the shape of their teams. Every hire now feels more consequential. Budget scrutiny is higher. Organizational priorities shift faster. Leaders are asking whether AI changes the shape of the team before approving additional headcount. Even after interviews begin, circumstances can legitimately change. These higher stakes and wide-ranging unknowns slow down the process and make it more likely roles will be closed without being filled.

We have three groups, making their best effort in a challenging and changing environment. Each action and reaction is rational, but together they damage the system as a whole. Each one erodes trust. Everyone protects themselves, and everyone experiences a worse hiring process. A process that feels less human.

I don’t think there is one true answer to fix the situation, but empathy provides a path to reduce harm.
* *Candidates:* understand that recruiters are doing their best to manage hundreds, if not thousands, of resumes, including fabricated identities and misleading applications
* *Recruiters:* remember that most resumes come from real people who want to work, even if the use of poor AI-driven personalization is misguided
* *Hiring managers:* remember that late stage rejections are particularly costly to candidates, recruiters, and your own team

Trust has once again become the most precious resource in hiring.


*How are you adapting?*
