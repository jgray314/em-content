# Applying the Hiring Funnel

One of the perspective flips that is most surprising when you become a hiring manager is getting to see the entire hiring funnel for a role and being encouraged to think in terms of it.

As a hiring manager, you are aiming to make an offer and have it accepted and your efforts and those of your recruiter are pointed to that goal. But the expectation is that you are tracking your progress towards that goal and actively debugging the process. How is that done? The hiring funnel.

| Stage | Volume |
|---|---|
| Accept | 1 |
| Offers | 1-2 |
| Full Loops | 4-8 |
| Technical Screens | 6-18 |
| Hiring Manager and/or Recruiter Screens | 9-90 |
| Referrals | 20+ |
| Applications | 100+ (more typically 3-5x this number of referrals or applications) |

*An example hiring funnel*

## The Hiring Funnel and Its Use In Companies

The hiring funnel breaks the process into stages and tracks progression between those stages in comparison to benchmarks to debug potential problems.

While beneficial for individual roles it becomes even more important in looking across an organization. Is a company's compensation competitive for a role? Well if they have less than half their offers accepted, something is up and compensation should be checked out. Are their early stage screenings effective? If less than 10% of candidates are clearing final rounds, they may be using too loose of standards. More than 75% passing final rounds, they may be rejecting too many good candidates with the earlier screening. Abysmal rates of progression from recruiter and hiring manager screens? Time to look at your job description for clarity and how you are promoting a role.

As a hiring manager or recruiter the funnel also helps you understand your candidate pool and track your progress to bringing someone in. Have 3x the number of post-recruiter-screen candidates as expected for an accept and early candidates are tracking the expected progression? Turn off the JD to focus on the pool you have. Trying to plan for next quarter? Your ATS will give you a surprisingly good estimate of when you can expect someone to start based on the role top of funnel and company wide metrics.

The hiring funnel also makes very explicit how quickly the field narrows and the increased investment appropriate for each stage of bringing a candidate through the process. By the time a hiring manager is ready to make an offer, they and the company have made a meaningful investment in the process, not just time with the offered candidate, but the time spent with all the unsuccessful candidates.

## Takeaways for Job Searchers

1. First and foremost, there is always an element of a numbers game in interviewing and hiring. These numbers are for roles that are actually filled by an external candidate. Not roles that are closed unfilled or filled by internal candidates. Many roles are looking at well over 100 resumes before reaching a hire and even with referrals it takes a few dozen.

2. Second, you too can use the funnel in evaluating your search progress or lack thereof and figuring out where to invest in improving it. Having a pass rate of 50% for a given stage of the process is average to very good actually.

3. Third, at a steady state, not all stages of the process deserve your same level of attention. Final rounds are very important, an initial screening call less so. When you have to make tradeoff decisions with your time, you should take that into account.

4. Fourth, referrals provide a 3-5x lift versus applications, but are far from a guarantee for a call, much less an offer.
